# AVantage — Product Requirements

**PRD v1.0 · September 2026**

Autonomous Fleet Revenue Intelligence — an open-source, interactive fleet simulation
and optimization platform demonstrating how autonomous vehicle operators can improve
contribution margin through smarter dispatch and repositioning.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Demonstration city | New York City |
| Fleet | 100 synthetic AVs |
| Primary objective | Contribution margin |
| Optimization | Dispatch + repositioning |
| Development | OpenCode + GitHub |
| Deployment | Railway |

## Product thesis

Fleet profitability is not simply a function of the number of vehicles or rides. It
also depends on which requests are served, how far vehicles travel empty, where idle
capacity is positioned, and whether those decisions compromise future service.

AVantage makes those trade-offs visible through a real optimization engine, a
simulated fleet moving across an interactive NYC map, and a reproducible financial
model. This is a portfolio product, not a claim to reproduce any operator's
proprietary dispatch system.

The strongest demonstration is a controlled experiment: give a baseline dispatcher and
an optimized dispatcher the same fleet, demand, prices, travel times, and operating
assumptions, then compare results.

## 1. Product requirements

### 1.1 The problem

An AV operator must repeatedly decide whether to assign an available vehicle to a
passenger, reposition it toward anticipated demand, or leave it in place. Each choice
has competing consequences:

| Decision | Potential benefit | Potential cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept a ride | Fare revenue | Pickup distance and vehicle commitment |
| Reposition | Better future availability | Empty miles and energy |
| Remain idle | Avoid unnecessary travel | Missed future demand |
| Prioritize high-value trips | Higher revenue | Reduced service coverage |
| Expand fleet | More capacity | Higher operating costs |

A conventional nearest-vehicle dispatcher considers immediate pickup distance but may
overlook the opportunity cost of sending a vehicle away from an area with high future
demand. AVantage tests whether demand-aware optimization can improve the resulting
economics.

### 1.2 Target users

Primary: fleet operations, marketplace optimization, autonomous mobility, and
operations research teams. Secondary: mobility researchers, transportation planners,
and technical hiring managers evaluating applied optimization work.

### 1.3 Success criteria

The application must demonstrate that it can:

- Reconstruct geographic and temporal demand patterns from a documented dataset.
- Generate a reproducible hypothetical fleet with realistic operational states.
- Animate vehicle movements and trip events on a real street map.
- Run a mathematically defined dispatch and repositioning policy.
- Compare that policy against a credible baseline using identical conditions.
- Calculate contribution margin from explicit revenue and cost assumptions.
- Explain individual optimization decisions and their constraints.
- Export results so another person can reproduce the experiment.

**Release gate:** the product is not complete until its optimization results are
generated by a functioning solver and independently checked against the simulation
event log.

## 2. Data strategy

Sources: NYC TLC trip records (demand, duration, distance, fares), NYC taxi zones
(geographic boundaries), OpenStreetMap (street network), OSRM (precomputed travel
times), a deterministic synthetic fleet generator, and optional Open-Meteo weather.

Initial reproducible release: one documented month, Manhattan only, airports excluded,
one representative weekday, 5-minute demand intervals, origin/destination zone, request
time, duration, distance, and fare proxy. Yellow taxi records first (trip-level fare
fields); high-volume for-hire data added later as a separate calibration experiment.

> Taxi trips are a proxy for potential AV demand, not observed AV demand. The assumed
> fraction of historical demand captured by the hypothetical AV service is adjustable.

Heavy preprocessing runs locally with DuckDB; a compact redistributable sample is
committed (or reconstructed by a script). Full-year TLC data is never processed during
Railway startup.

## 3. Synthetic fleet

100 hypothetical electric AVs with explicit states — `idle`, `pickup`, `reposition`,
`on_trip`, `charging`. Battery state of charge decreases with distance; vehicles below
the operating threshold are unavailable until charging completes. The charging rule is
identical across dispatch strategies.

## 4. Optimization

Two-stage rolling-horizon approach every five simulated minutes: (1) dispatch
assignments, (2) repositioning of remaining idle vehicles based on forecast demand.
See [OPTIMIZATION.md](OPTIMIZATION.md) for the full formulation. Solved with Google
OR-Tools.

## 5. Revenue and contribution margin

`CM = revenue − energy − distance − time − transaction costs`. Revenue is recognized
only for completed rides. Fixed ownership costs are reported separately as an optional
fleet-level operating-profit scenario. Contribution margin is **not** net profit.

## 6. Baseline and evaluation

Baseline: **Nearest Feasible** — assign each request to the closest available eligible
vehicle, respect pickup ETA and battery constraints, process chronologically, no
proactive repositioning. A second baseline adds demand-proportional repositioning to
isolate dispatch vs. repositioning effects.

Evaluation: identical scenarios, common random seeds, ≥20 independent demand seeds,
paired differences reported. Primary KPI: contribution margin per vehicle-hour.
Secondary KPIs: demand fulfillment, average/P90 pickup ETA, empty-mile ratio,
utilization, revenue per vehicle-hour, service coverage, energy consumption. Unserved
demand is reported alongside margin. No improvement is guaranteed.

## 7. Experience

Map-centric public app: play/pause, speed (1×/5×/20×/60×), scrub, demand heatmap
toggle, repositioning-route toggle, vehicle selection, baseline/optimized switch, and
synchronized financial metrics. The default demo uses precomputed scenarios; a
separate bounded "Run Custom Scenario" action can execute optimization requests.
